How to Tell If Your Diesel Fuel Is Contaminated

Published: June 5, 2026 | 5 min read

Warning Signs of Diesel Fuel Contamination

Diesel fuel contamination is a silent problem. It develops slowly over time, often without noticeable symptoms until your equipment fails. In Florida's hot, humid climate, fuel contamination can develop in as little as 3-6 months. Here's what to look for.

1. Frequent Fuel Filter Clogging

If you're changing fuel filters more often than usual, it's a strong indicator of contamination. Black sludge, algae, and sediment build up in your tank and clog filters. If a new filter clogs within hours or days, your fuel is contaminated.

2. Black Smoke from Exhaust

Dirty fuel doesn't burn efficiently. Black or dark smoke from your generator, boat engine, or truck exhaust indicates incomplete combustion caused by water, sludge, or microbial contamination in the fuel.

3. Hard Starting or Rough Idling

Engines that struggle to start, run rough at idle, or stall unexpectedly may be suffering from contaminated fuel. Water in diesel fuel kills injector performance and disrupts combustion.

4. Visible Sludge or Growth

If you can see dark sludge at the bottom of your fuel tank or fuzzy growth on the tank walls, you have microbial contamination. This "diesel bug" is a combination of bacteria, fungi, and algae that thrive in diesel fuel.

5. Water in Your Fuel

Water is the #1 cause of diesel fuel problems. Check your fuel/water separator. If you're draining water frequently, condensation is building up in your tank. In Florida's humid climate, this is a constant battle.
