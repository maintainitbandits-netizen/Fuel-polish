Why Florida Generators Need Annual Fuel Polishing

Published: May 28, 2026 | 6 min read

NFPA 110 Compliance in Florida's Climate

For hospitals, data centers, and commercial buildings in Florida, emergency generators are life-saving equipment. But Florida's tropical climate creates unique challenges for diesel fuel storage that require regular attention.

The Florida Factor

Florida's average humidity of 75-85% means every diesel fuel tank experiences condensation. Warm temperatures accelerate microbial growth. The result? Diesel fuel can become contaminated within 3-6 months of storage, even in a sealed tank.

NFPA 110 Requirements

NFPA 110, the Standard for Emergency and Standby Power Systems, requires that fuel be maintained in a usable condition. Regular fuel testing and polishing are essential for compliance. Facilities that fail to maintain clean fuel risk citation and, worse, generator failure during emergencies.

What Happens Without Annual Polishing

  • Microbial growth clogs fuel filters within hours of generator start
  • Water accumulation causes injector damage and corrosion
  • Fuel oxidation creates varnish that gums up fuel systems
  • Generator fails to start during power outage or hurricane
  • Costly emergency repairs and fuel disposal required
